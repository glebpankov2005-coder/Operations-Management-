## 0. The 60-second version — remember this

1. **What goes out.** About **15 trailers a day**. **73% carry parcels only**; an average trailer takes **1.2 pallets + 15 cartons**.
2. **Today.** Trailers stand at the doors all day (up to **15 at once**). Only **49.9%** of lines are staged on time, and only **37%** of orders leave within 24 hours.
3. **Our design: a fixed departure plan.**
   - **14 trailer slots a day**, each with a time and a door.
   - The 3 picking waves are planned **backwards** from those times, so every wave ends **at least 45 minutes before** its trailers leave.
   - Orders received **before 12:00** leave **the same day**.
4. **Result.**
   - **65%** of orders out within 24 hours (was 37%).
   - The slowest 10% wait **70 h** instead of 102 h.
   - At most **4 of the 5 doors** are busy at once.
5. **Value for DHL.**
   - **€101k a year** less labour.
   - The process changes cost **€157k** and pay back in **~1.6 years**.
   - Cost: **€3.09 per order line**.
   - The full design costs **€0.52M** (re-using today's racks) or **€1.63M** (new racks).

## 3. How shipping works in our design

### 3.1 Five shipping doors (S1–S5) on the east wall

**How we sized the doors:**
- Take the **busiest hour** (p90 of the days): **9 trailers** leave.
- Each one is at the door for about **17.5 minutes** on average (15 min docking and paperwork + loading its pallets and roll cages with the ERE 225). The reports round this to 18.
- A door can be used **85%** of the hour.
- **9 × 17.5 ÷ (60 × 0.85) = 3.1 doors**, so we need **4**, **+ 1 spare = 5**. (With the rounded 18 minutes you get 3.2, still 4 doors.)

**Fixed time slots are a condition of this design:**

| Situation | Doors needed (p90) |
|---|---|
| Today: trailers parked all day | ~15 |
| Future without time slots (live loading, but no booked times) | 8 |
| **Future with time slots** | **3.1 → 4 + 1 spare = 5** |

### 3.2 The departure plan

Each carrier gets fixed departure slots, set close to that carrier's **current median departure time** (measured), so carriers barely have to change.

| Departure | Carrier | Trailers | Fed by wave |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10:30 | Ex-works pickup (morning) | 1 | W1 |
| 11:00 | DHL DSV run 1 | 1 | W1 |
| 13:30 | Ex-works pickup (afternoon) | 1 | W2 |
| 14:00 | DHL Parcel NL run 1 | 1 | W2 |
| 14:00 | Ex-works pickup (afternoon) | 1 | W2 |
| 14:30 | DHL DSV run 2 | 1 | W2 |
| 15:00 | Customer carriers | 2 | W2 |
| 16:00 | DHL Parcel NL DACH | 1 | W3 |
| 16:15 | DHL Parcel NL run 2 | 1 | W3 |
| 16:15 | DHL4YOU | 1 | W3 |
| 16:30 | DHL Connect & Return | 1 | W3 |
| 16:30 | DHL Parcel BE & LU | 1 | W3 |
| 16:45 | DHL Parcel EDC | 1 | W3 |
| | **Total** | **14** | |

**The waves** (Arnau's picking):

| Wave | Time | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | 06:00–09:30 | afternoon orders from the day before |
| W2 | 09:30–12:45 | the rest of the backlog + morning orders |
| W3 | 12:45–15:15 | orders received before 12:00, which leave the same day |

- Every wave ends **at least 45 minutes** before the trailers it feeds, which leaves time to pack and stage.
- **Same-day service** becomes possible: **32%** of orders arrive before 12:00 and can leave on that afternoon's parcel trailers through wave 3.
- **Slots are 25 minutes.** At most **4 of the 5 doors** are in use at once, so one stays free for ex-works pickups that come late or early.

### 3.3 From picking to the trailer

**Small orders (84% of orders):**
1. Picked in batches on a trolley.
2. Sorted per order at the **put-to-light wall** (120 slots).
3. Packed at one of **4 pack stations** (bench, scale, label printer).
4. Placed in the **roll cage** of their carrier.
5. The roll cage waits in the **staging lane** of its door.

**Large orders (16% of orders, 88% of the volume):**
1. Picked straight onto a pallet with the EJE 120i.
2. Dropped at packing, checked and **stretch-wrapped**, and labelled.
3. Put in the staging lane of their door.

**Loading:**
- Trailers are loaded **live** at their booked time with the **ERE 225** rider pallet truck: about 1.2 minutes per pallet (48 an hour).
- Goods wait **on the floor** in the staging lane, not in a parked trailer.

| Put-to-light wall (120 slots, 10.8 m, working strip both sides) | 39 |

**Outbound staging (zone 6): 216 m²**
- One lane per shipping door.
- Busy day (p90): ~18 pallets and ~14 roll cages waiting, about 60 m².
- Busiest afternoon in the data: about 100 m², which is **46%** of the area.
- So staging always fits.

**Packing staff:**
- About **45 lines an hour** per packer.
- That gives **14 packing hours** on an average day and **26** on the busiest day: about 3.5 packers, which the **4 pack stations** cover.

### 3.5 How shipping is planned and controlled

| Activity | How | Why (data) |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling | a fixed departure plan: 14 trailer slots booked by the carriers between 10:30 and 16:45 | ~15 trailers a day; p90 9 in the busiest hour |
| Loading (people) | packers per wave from the packing lines (~45 lines an hour per packer); 4 pack stations | 14 packing hours on an average day, 26 on the busiest |
| Order of work | pack and stage in the order of the departure slots; each door has its own staging lane; trailers loaded live at the booked time | 73% of trailers carry parcels only |
| Control | on-time shipment, orders out within 24 h and trailers at the doors, checked after each departure | a missed slot moves to the carrier's next slot the same day |

**Every day:** a short start-of-day meeting with the four owners: yesterday's KPIs, today's bookings, wave sizes, staff and departures. **Every week:** a KPI review with the DHL operations manager. **Every month:** a review with Assa Abloy.

| # | Step | Calculation | Result |

## 8. Questions you may get — and answers

**"Is 98% on time a calculated result?"**
No, it's a target. Every wave ends at least 45 minutes before its trailers leave, so the plan is built for it. Today's 49.9% is measured. The 65% within 24 hours is calculated: we re-timed all 8,774 real orders.

**"How did you get 65%?"**
- We gave each real order a new departure time: its wave depends on when it arrived; it is ready 45 minutes after the wave ends; then it leaves on the first slot of its own carrier.
- Then we compared the new departure time with the arrival time. 65% leave within 24 hours.

**"Why does the median only go from 25 to 22 hours?"**
Most orders arrive between 15:00 and 17:00 and still leave the next day. The gain is that no order waits a second or third day, so the slowest 10% go from 102 to 70 hours.

**"What if the carriers don't accept fixed slots?"**
- Slots are a condition of the design. Without them, up to 8 doors would be needed.
- We set each slot close to that carrier's current median departure time, so for them little changes.
- It's one of the three decisions we ask from DHL.

**"What if a trailer is late or misses its slot?"**
It moves to the carrier's next slot the same day. One door is always free, because at most 4 of the 5 are busy at once.

**"Why 5 doors?"**
In the busiest hour 9 trailers leave, each at the door for about 17.5 minutes, and a door is used 85% of the time: 9 × 17.5 ÷ 51 = 3.1, so 4 doors, plus 1 spare.

**"Why load live instead of parking trailers?"**
Parked trailers block the doors all day, up to 15 at once. With live loading at booked times the goods wait in the staging lane, and at most 4 doors are busy.

**"How did you get €101k?"**
- 19.4 picker-hours a day less: 12.1 from batching and the fast-pick area, 7.4 from no peak staffing.
- Divide by 7.5 productive hours, multiply by 8 paid hours and 244 working days: about 5,060 paid hours a year. At €20, that's €101,208.

**"Why €20 an hour?"**
That's the figure from our course teacher for a warehouse operator. We tested €28 as a high case: it adds about €86k a year.

**"A 16-year payback — is that worth it?"**
- The racks aren't there to save labour; they're needed to hold the stock at all. So we judge them on cost per place (€4.49 per place a month).
- The labour saving comes from the process changes, which pay back in 1.6 years and can start first.
- If today's racks can be re-used, the full design costs €0.52M (5 years).

**"Are these real prices?"**
No, they're benchmark prices from public sources, each with a low–high range in our cost register. The next step is quotes for racking, carton flow, the put-to-light wall and the truck lease.

**"How did you get €3.09 per order line?"**
Running cost €341,548 plus depreciation €141,591 = €483,139 a year, divided by 156,160 order lines a year (640 a day × 244 days).

**"What's not in the cost?"**
The building, dock levellers, sprinklers and the hazardous-goods compartment. They're outside the project scope.

**"What happens if volume grows by 50%?"**
One extra reach truck and one extra pallet truck. The same 5 doors are enough, though all 5 are then busy in the busiest hour. At double volume the doors become the limit.

**"Why are some countries so slow?"**
Switzerland and Poland take 5–6 days today. That points at customs or weekly consolidation for export, a question for DHL.

**"Why trucks on lease and not bought?"**
A full-service lease includes repairs, so a breakdown is the lessor's problem. It's a running cost (€35,400 a year), and the price hardly affects the total: only 7 trucks.

## 11. Glossary — the words in plain language

### Shipping and money words

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Carrier | the transport company that takes the goods (DHL Parcel, DSV, …) |
| Ex-works | the customer collects the goods with their own transport |
| Parcel only | a trailer that takes cartons but no pallet |
| Time slot / departure slot | a booked time and door for a trailer |
| Live loading | the trailer is loaded at the door at its booked time and then leaves |
| Parked (drop) trailer | a trailer left at a door all day and loaded whenever goods are ready (today's way) |
| Roll cage | a metal cage on wheels for cartons and parcels |
| Lead time | the time from order received until the trailer leaves |
| Staged on time | the order line is in staging before its trailer's planned departure; the WMS flags the others "LATE" |
| Same day | the order leaves on the day it was received |
| Pack station | a bench with a scale and a label printer where orders are packed |
| Door use 85% | a door can't be busy every minute, so we plan to use 85% of each hour |
| Contingency | an extra 10% on the investment for things we can't foresee |
| Full-service lease | renting a truck per month with repairs included |
| Benchmark price vs quote | benchmark = a typical price from a public source; quote = an actual offer from a supplier |
| Productive vs paid hours | a person is paid for 8 hours a day but works about 7.5 productive hours (breaks and so on) |
| Cost per order line | the full yearly cost (running cost + depreciation) divided by the order lines in a year |
| Simple payback | investment ÷ saving per year, ignoring interest |

### General words (the same in all four briefings)

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| WMS | the warehouse management system: the computer system that knows where every pallet is and tells people what to do. Almost all our data comes from it |
| Item (SKU) | one specific product, e.g. one type of lock. There are 5,291 in stock |
| Load | one pallet (or other carrier) of stock with its own number in the WMS |
| Order / order line | an order is what one customer asks for; each different item on it is one order line |
| Average vs median | the average adds everything up and divides; the median is the middle value (half are lower, half higher), so a few extreme values don't pull it up |
| p90 / p95 | the value that 90% (or 95%) of cases stay under. "The slowest 10%" means everything above p90 |
| 1.83× (peak factor) | the busiest day divided by an average day |
| FTE | full-time equivalent: one person working a full day (7.5 productive hours out of 8 paid) |
| Picker-hours | hours of picking work in a day; 11.7 picker-hours is about 1.6 people |
| KPI | key performance indicator: a number we track to see if the operation works as planned |
| Measured / modelled / assumption / benchmark / target | measured = straight from Assa Abloy's data; modelled = our calculation on that data; assumption = a value we chose and wrote down with its reason; benchmark = a typical value from a public source; target = what the design is built to reach |
| Assumption register | our list of 46 assumptions (data/assumptions.json), each with its reason, source and impact |
| Option A / B / C | the three designs we compared: A = today's way tidied up; B = hybrid (chosen); C = semi-automated |
| Four V's | volume, variety, variation and visibility: four ways to describe an operation (Slack et al., 2022) |
| Fast-pick area | the zone along the south wall where the 1,409 best-sellers each have a pick face, close to packing |
| Reserve (pallet racks) | the high racks where full pallets are stored until they are needed |
| Single-deep rack | a rack with one pallet behind each face, so every pallet can be taken out without moving another |
| Wave | a portion of the day's orders released to the floor together; we use 3 a day |
| Put-to-light wall | a wall of 120 slots with lights at packing; it sorts batch-picked items into their orders |
| Staging lane | floor area in front of a shipping door where goods wait for their trailer |
| R1–R3 / S1–S5 | the receiving doors (west wall) / the shipping doors (east wall) |
| ETV 216i | Jungheinrich reach truck: puts pallets away in the racks (lifts up to 10.7 m) and refills the fast-pick area |
| EJE 120i | Jungheinrich walkie pallet truck (the operator walks with it): used to pick large orders onto a pallet |
| ERE 225 | Jungheinrich rider pallet truck (the operator stands on it): used to unload and load trailers |
| CAPEX | one-off investment: racks, equipment, set-up |
| OPEX | running cost every year: staff, truck lease, energy, upkeep |
| Depreciation | the investment spread over the years it lasts (racks 15 years, equipment 7, IT 5) |
| Payback | how many years of savings it takes to earn back an investment |
| Sensitivity | how much the result changes if an assumption is wrong (e.g. labour at €28 instead of €20 an hour) |
| Stress test | checking the design at 150%, 200% and 250% of today's volume |
